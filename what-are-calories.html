What are calories, really?

Strip away the diet marketing and a calorie is just a unit of energy. This page gives you the ten-minute version of everything you need to know before you start counting.

The definition

A calorie measures energy, not evil

A calorie (technically a kilocalorie, written kcal) is the amount of energy needed to raise the temperature of one kilogram of water by one degree Celsius. That's it. When a label says a banana has 105 calories, it means eating it delivers that much usable energy to your body.

Your body burns a small but constant stream of this energy just to stay alive — keeping your heart beating, your lungs working and your cells repairing themselves. Everything beyond that, from walking to the shop to lifting weights, spends a little more. Add up what you take in and what you burn and you get the balance that decides whether your weight goes up, down or stays flat.

Same word, different numbers

kcal vs. Cal vs. calorie

kcalWhat food labels actually mean. 1 kcal = 1,000 small calories. The "calories" on a label are always kcal.
Cal (capital C)Nutrition shorthand for kcal. A label saying "Calories: 200" means 200 kcal.
kJKilojoules, the metric equivalent. 1 kcal ≈ 4.18 kJ. Europe labels in kJ; the number will look about four times bigger.

Where the energy comes from

Four nutrients carry the calories

Only three things you eat give you energy (plus alcohol, which gives you energy but nothing else). Here's what each gram is worth.

NutrientEnergy per gramFound inRole
Carbohydrates4 kcalGrains, fruit, vegetables, sugarThe body's preferred fast fuel.
Protein4 kcalMeat, fish, eggs, dairy, beansBuilds and repairs tissue; also satiating.
Fat9 kcalOils, butter, nuts, fatty fishDense, slow-burning fuel and cell structure.
Alcohol7 kcalDrinksEnergy with no nutritional value.

This is why fat is the easiest way to overshoot a calorie budget — it delivers more than twice the energy of the same weight of carbs or protein.

Where the energy goes

How your body spends it

For a typical adult, the calories you burn each day split roughly like this:

~60–70%Basal metabolism — the energy to keep you alive at complete rest. The largest share, and largely out of your control.
~15–30%Activity — everything you move for. The most flexible share, and the one exercise changes.
~10%Digesting food — called the thermic effect; simply processing what you ate burns a little energy.

This is why "calories out" can't be pinned to one number. Your resting burn is shaped by body size, muscle mass, age and genetics — that's what the calculator estimates.

Myths that waste your time

Common misconceptions

MYTH

"Calories from sugar make you fat; calories from healthy food don't." Reality: a calorie is a calorie at the balance level — but where it comes from changes how full you feel, how steady your energy is and how much you're likely to eat next. That's why quality still matters.

MYTH

"You can't trust label calories because they're all wrong." Reality: packaged-food labels are allowed small margins of error, and whole foods vary. But the difference is a few percent — nowhere near enough to explain a stalled diet. Consistency beats perfection.

MYTH

"Starvation mode means eating less does nothing." Reality: your body does adapt and burn slightly less as you lose weight — that's real. But it's a modest effect, not a shutdown. You don't stop losing; you just may need a small adjustment.
